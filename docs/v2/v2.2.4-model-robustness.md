# V2.2.4 Model uncertainty and robustness

## A. Objective

V2.2.4 answers how stable an accepted point-model output is under descriptive inspection of the exact bounded evidence that produced it. It adds provider-independent empirical pace spread and deterministic tyre-slope perturbation diagnostics. It does not add or change a pace or tyre model.

## B. Distinct semantics

A point estimate is the accepted single model output. `DataQuality` describes limitations in the evidence the model actually used. Empirical evidence spread describes the observed clean lap-time distribution. Robustness describes sensitivity of an accepted slope to deterministic one-observation perturbations. Confidence remains `UNKNOWN`; neither output assigns probability. These concepts are deliberately separate.

The existing `Uncertainty` contract is not populated. Pace quantiles are not a distribution over the true pace estimate, and the leave-one-out (LOO) envelope is not a probabilistic interval. Both V2.2.2 and V2.2.3 estimates therefore retain `uncertainty=None`.

## C. Frozen V2.2.2 semantics

Representative pace remains the standard-library median of eligible observations in the trailing eight-lap-number window, with a minimum of three clean laps, every factual exclusion applied and `WEATHER_TRANSITION` excluded. Availability, evidence window, diagnostics, confidence, value and model/config identity are unchanged. The accepted point config hash remains `sha256:ad8ba93d200ae7239d94a9ae471cbc8faaed3f2d3af3935c4e1723358fd8c7f0`.

## D. Frozen V2.2.3 semantics

Tyre-age slope remains closed-form OLS over exactly the latest canonical bounded stint: no fallback, pooling or unknown/mixed compound; consistent lap-for-lap age progression; at least five clean observations and a four-lap age span; factual exclusions and `WEATHER_TRANSITION` applied. Negative slopes remain valid. Availability, evidence window, compound, stint, counts, intercept, residuals, confidence, point value and model/config identity are unchanged. The accepted point config hash remains `sha256:20efe829455052c79230615eac810640f663c361313c7de44d9849190674b88c`.

## E. Architecture

The pure `app.models_v2.model_robustness` layer accepts only `PaceEstimationContext`, accepted typed point configuration and typed `ModelRobustnessConfig`. It calls the accepted point estimator first and embeds that immutable `point_fit` in each result. Pace selection was extracted unchanged into `select_pace_evidence`, shared by point and robustness paths. Tyre robustness uses the accepted fit's latest-stint identity and the existing V2.2.3 clean-lap selector, then reconciles candidate count, selection audit, sample count, tyre-age range and recomputed nominal OLS exactly before analysis.

The layer has competitor and canonically ordered full-field entry points. It contains no provider DTO, client, provider key, fetch path, V1 object, API or frontend dependency.

## F. Robustness configuration and versioning

The baseline is `empirical-model-robustness` version `0.1.0`, fixed quantiles `(0.1, 0.5, 0.9)`, typed `linear_interpolation`, and explicit tyre zero tolerance `1e-9 s/lap/lap`. Canonical JSON SHA-256 hashing covers every field. Its config hash is `sha256:f1728939dc8ff5ae30e23a0fbcc06dff2c2b44b4cb47e58d9dd5599d9d0b8032`; changing the tolerance or version changes identity without changing either point-model hash.

## G. Pace empirical quantiles

For an available point fit, `PaceRobustnessDiagnostics` carries typed method, `EmpiricalQuantiles`, included sample count, observed minimum/maximum and descriptive `p90 - p10` spread. Only the exact clean observations accepted by V2.2.2 enter the sorted values. An unavailable point fit produces unavailable robustness and preserves the point reason inside `point_fit`; the minimum evidence gate is never lowered.

## H. Exact interpolation formula

For sorted values `x[0] ... x[n-1]` and quantile `q`, set `position = (n - 1) * q`, `lower = floor(position)` and `upper = ceil(position)`. If the indices match, return `x[index]`. When the interpolation weight `position - lower` is exactly `0.5`, return `(x[lower] + x[upper]) / 2` to match the accepted standard-library median's floating-point arithmetic exactly; all other weights use `x[lower] + (x[upper] - x[lower]) * (position - lower)`. This remains linear interpolation, stays inside the observed range and preserves exact p50/point equality without a tolerance.

## I. Pace quantiles are not confidence intervals

P10, p50 and p90 describe only the empirical distribution of included observed lap times. They are not confidence bounds around the median, a forecast/prediction interval, a distribution of true car pace, or a probability statement. They do not resolve fuel, tyre, traffic, weather, track-evolution or driver-management confounding.

## J. Tyre leave-one-out method

For every available V2.2.3 fit, omit each accepted observation once and refit the identical closed-form OLS of lap time against tyre age. `TyreSlopeRobustnessDiagnostics` records the nominal slope/sign, explicit tolerance, every omitted lap and refit slope/sign, refit count, LOO minimum/maximum, sign counts, sign stability, maximum absolute deviation and deterministic most-influential omission. Tied maximum deviations choose the lowest canonical lap number.

## K. Diagnostic gate semantics

LOO subsets are diagnostic perturbations, not alternative production estimates, so the five-clean-lap and four-lap-age-span production gates are not reapplied. Every refit must still contain at least two observations, two distinct ages and a non-zero OLS denominator. If not, robustness is explicitly unavailable with `INVALID_DIAGNOSTIC_REFIT`; no failed perturbation is ignored and no partial range is presented.

## L. Sensitivity envelope

The typed `StatisticalInterval` lower bound is `min(nominal, all LOO slopes)` and the upper bound is `max(nominal, all LOO slopes)`. `coverage=None` and method is `leave-one-observation-out deterministic sensitivity envelope`. This numerical envelope is not a confidence, prediction, probability or posterior interval.

## M. Slope sign tolerance

The explicit baseline tolerance is `1e-9 s/lap/lap`. A slope strictly greater than tolerance is `POSITIVE`; strictly less than negative tolerance is `NEGATIVE`; both exact boundaries and all values between them are `ZERO`. The tolerance is present in typed diagnostics and robustness config provenance, never hidden.

## N. Sign stability

`sign_stable=true` only when every LOO refit's tolerance-classified sign equals the nominal slope's sign. A zero or opposite-sign refit makes it false. This is the only categorical robustness result: no arbitrary magnitude threshold or generic robust/fragile grade exists, and sign counts are descriptive rather than probabilities.

## O. DataQuality

Every robustness result exactly preserves the accepted point fit's model-relevant `DataQuality`; contract validation rejects substitution and requires `completeness=None`. Computation cannot repair missing evidence or improve quality. Point-unavailable results preserve the point diagnostic quality, and mathematically invalid perturbations are separately typed.

## P. Provenance

Deterministic model provenance records exact selected-evidence reuse, interpolation or one-observation omission, accepted OLS reuse, explicit tolerance and non-probabilistic envelope semantics. It carries point and robustness config hashes without fabricated source timestamps (`observed_at=None`). Nested point fits preserve their own model provenance; all underlying source observations remain bounded by `PaceEstimationContext.as_of`, and contract validators reject future-observed robustness provenance.

## Q. No-hindsight

The robustness layer cannot fetch or recover rejected evidence. Synthetic provider contamination tests add future laps, stints and weather to otherwise identical inputs and prove byte/model equality across provider transformation, canonical context, V2.2.2 point output, V2.2.3 point output and both V2.2.4 outputs at the same cutoff. At a later cutoff the selected evidence and robustness may legitimately change; the earlier context and outputs remain immutable and contain no later observation.

## T. Known limitations

Empirical pace spread mixes all unresolved state inside the accepted window. LOO measures influence of individual accepted observations but cannot identify omitted variables, model misspecification or a causal physical degradation rate. Small samples remain small; a stable sign does not establish magnitude accuracy. No Gaussian, t, Bayesian, bootstrap, p-value, R-squared trust, standard-deviation confidence or probability interpretation is available.

## U. Explicit non-goals

V2.2.4 adds no new estimator, alternate aggregation, fuel correction, track-evolution correction, traffic model, outlier classifier, slope clamp, fallback, stint pooling, probabilistic interval, confidence score, strategy recommendation, public modelling API or frontend change. It adds no `TyreAgeSlopeEstimate` alias; `TyreDegradationEstimate` remains for compatibility. V2.2.5, V2.3 and V2.4 remain unstarted.

## V. Proposed V2.2.5 presentation contract

An operator view should consume the typed results directly. Representative Pace may show the unchanged point, labelled empirical clean-lap p10/p50/p90, sample count, observed range, p90-p10 spread and quality. Tyre Behaviour may show the unchanged empirical point slope, compound/stint, age span, deterministic LOO envelope, sign stable/unstable, most influential omitted lap and quality; unavailable results should show the preserved typed point reason. UI copy must avoid confidence/probability language, surface limitations, and never derive semantics from provenance strings.
