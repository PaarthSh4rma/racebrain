# V2.2.3 Tyre-Age Pace Slope

## Objective and exact semantics

V2.2.3 estimates the empirical change in observed lap time associated with one additional lap of canonical tyre age inside one competitor's latest bounded stint. The unit is `s/lap/lap`. Positive, zero and negative values are valid: a positive value means observed laps became slower with age, while a negative value means they became faster.

The coefficient is descriptive. It is not automatically physical tyre degradation, intrinsic compound performance, a fuel-corrected loss, clean-air degradation or a causal tyre effect. Fuel burn, track evolution and other time-varying conditions are not separately identifiable from the accepted evidence.

## Provider-independent architecture

The pure model service in `app.models_v2.tyre_age_slope` consumes only immutable `PaceEstimationContext` and typed `TyreSlopeModelConfig`. It has independent competitor and full-field entry points and no client, DTO, provider key, raw-response, V1 simulation, API or frontend dependency. The OpenF1 adapter remains solely responsible for producing canonical bounded observations.

## Latest-cohort policy

Each canonical competitor receives exactly one deterministic `CompetitorTyreSlopeFit`. The selected cohort is identified by the competitor's latest admitted lap:

1. the latest lap must have a known compound, tyre age and bounded stint number;
2. candidates are that competitor's observations with the same stint number;
3. every candidate must have complete tyre annotation and one common identified compound;
4. ordered candidate tyre age must advance by exactly the corresponding lap-number delta;
5. no earlier stint is substituted if the latest cohort is missing, ambiguous, short or otherwise unidentifiable.

This policy does not pool unrelated stint intercepts, mix compounds or cherry-pick an older successful fit. A stale competitor is evaluated against its own latest bounded evidence. `UNKNOWN` is not an identified compound.

## Baseline configuration and identifiability

The first baseline is explicit and versioned:

- model `empirical-tyre-age-slope`, version `0.1.0`
- minimum 5 selected clean laps
- minimum included tyre-age span 4 laps
- excluded warning `WEATHER_TRANSITION`

The minimums are conservative initial model parameters, not Formula 1 constants. A preliminary live audit showed that four points admitted a leverage-sensitive Monaco cohort with slope `-5.2328 s/lap/lap`: its clean ages/times were `2/97.899`, `4/76.561`, `5/76.567`, `6/77.829`, after a pit-out exclusion at age 3. Requiring five clean points prevents that weak cohort from being presented without adding an outlier classifier, a slope threshold or a clamp. Robustness analysis remains V2.2.4 work.

`TyreSlopeModelConfig.version()` SHA-256 hashes its complete canonical JSON representation. Changing sample count, age span, warning policy or version changes config identity. The approved baseline hash is `sha256:20efe829455052c79230615eac810640f663c361313c7de44d9849190674b88c`.

## Selection and unavailable results

Within the selected cohort, every factual `LapExclusionReason` is disqualifying. Only quality warnings configured for exclusion are disqualifying; the baseline excludes weather transitions but does not infer or automatically exclude traffic or statistical outliers. `DataQuality` level itself never filters a lap.

Typed unavailable reasons are:

- `NO_OBSERVATIONS`
- `MISSING_TYRE_ANNOTATION`
- `UNKNOWN_COMPOUND`
- `MIXED_COMPOUND`
- `INCONSISTENT_TYRE_AGE_PROGRESSION`
- `INSUFFICIENT_CLEAN_LAPS`
- `INSUFFICIENT_TYRE_AGE_SPAN`

No result is fabricated, no other competitor is substituted, and the estimator does not fall back to an earlier stint. Structurally invalid candidates are rejected as a cohort; ordinary hard/warning exclusions retain a typed per-reason audit. Overlapping reasons exclude one observation while preserving every encountered reason count.

## Estimator mathematics

For included canonical pairs `(x_i, y_i)` where `x` is tyre age in laps and `y` is lap time in seconds, closed-form ordinary least squares fits:

`y_i = intercept + slope × x_i + residual_i`

with:

`slope = Σ((x_i-x̄)(y_i-ȳ)) / Σ((x_i-x̄)²)`

and:

`intercept = ȳ - slope × x̄`

The identifiability gates guarantee a non-zero denominator. The implementation uses only Python's standard library. It performs no fixed fuel, track, traffic or tyre correction and never clamps negative slopes.

## Result and diagnostics contracts

`FieldTyreSlopeResult` contains one canonically ordered result per `RaceState` competitor. An available result wraps the existing `TyreDegradationEstimate`, whose exact interpretation is the empirical within-cohort tyre-age pace slope described above.

`TyreSlopeDiagnostics` extends reconciled fit diagnostics with compound, stint number, minimum/maximum tyre age, age span and fitted intercept. Candidate count means observations in the selected bounded stint; included count means clean observations used by OLS; excluded count means candidate observations rejected by selection. Included plus excluded equals candidates. The estimate sample count, subject, cutoff, version, compound, stint and tyre-age range must agree with diagnostics.

Residual diagnostics are the median fitted residual and `median(abs(residual))` in seconds. The evidence window uses the actual included laps and completion timestamps. Median absolute residual is descriptive, not sigma.

## DataQuality and provenance

Available result quality aggregates only model-relevant limitations from included observations: timing warnings, missing tyre annotation, missing race-control context used by mandatory control exclusions, and missing weather context when weather-transition exclusion is configured. Missing gap evidence does not propagate because the model does not use it. Quality limitations do not change selection, `completeness=None`, and current OpenF1 fits are `DEGRADED` because completion timestamps are reconstructed from `date_start + lap_duration`.

Unavailable fits use `INSUFFICIENT` quality and deterministic explanations while retaining relevant included-evidence limitations where a cohort was selectable.

Estimate and diagnostics provenance states that the latest canonical bounded stint was selected, factual/configured exclusions were applied, and closed-form OLS was used. It records model version and config hash without fabricating source timestamps. Existing cutoff validators reject future-observed provenance.

## No-hindsight and determinism

Every input observation was already bounded by V2.2.1. V2.2.3 is a pure function of context plus config, has no fetch path and never mutates input. Synthetic provider→context→slope tests prove that adding future provider rows leaves both context and complete slope output identical at the same cutoff. A later cutoff may legitimately select or extend a different latest cohort; the earlier immutable result remains unchanged.

## Live Monaco acceptance

Audited 11 September 2026 against session 9523 with Leclerc #16.

| Cutoff | Available / unavailable | Unavailable reasons | Candidate distribution | Included distribution | Slope min / median / max |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| lap 20 | 0 / 20 | missing annotation 15; no observations 4; insufficient clean laps 1 | 0×19, 5×1 | 0×19, 4×1 | none |
| lap 50 | 1 / 19 | missing annotation 14; no observations 4; insufficient clean laps 1 | 0×18, 1×1, 34×1 | 0×18, 1×1, 33×1 | `-0.0199203` |

Leclerc is unavailable at both cutoffs because the latest admitted lap lacks a complete canonical compound/age/stint annotation. That is the expected conservative response to Monaco's conflicting untimestamped restart-stint records. No annotation rule was weakened. At lap 20, one other car's five-lap cohort had one pit-out exclusion and only four clean laps, so the leverage-sensitive slope found during threshold inspection is not returned. At lap 50, the sole available 33-lap cohort is `DEGRADED`; all other results are `INSUFFICIENT`.

## Live Spain acceptance

Audited session 9539 with Norris #4.

| Cutoff | Available / unavailable | Unavailable reasons | Hard/configured exclusions | Slope min / median / max |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| lap 20 | 11 / 9 | insufficient clean laps 9 | lap one 4; pit out 16; weather transition 8 | `-0.0191190 / 0.0254746 / 0.3048571` |
| lap 50 | 14 / 6 | insufficient clean laps 6 | pit out 20; no configured-warning exclusions | `-0.1793091 / 0.0542338 / 0.3917000` |

At lap 20, Norris's latest cohort is soft stint 1. Candidate laps are 1–20; lap 1 is excluded as `LAP_ONE`, and laps 3–4 are excluded for `WEATHER_TRANSITION`. The 17 included ages span 2–20 and yield slope `-0.00956084 s/lap/lap`, intercept `81.175723 s`, median residual `0.053325 s`, residual MAD `0.293007 s`, and evidence laps 2–20. Negative is preserved because fuel burn/track evolution may outweigh empirical tyre-age association.

At lap 50, Norris's latest cohort is soft stint 3. Lap 48 is pit-out; only laps 49–50 remain, ages 2–3. The result is unavailable with `INSUFFICIENT_CLEAN_LAPS` rather than falling back to the long earlier medium stint. All available Spain fits are `DEGRADED` by approximate completion timing; unavailable fits are `INSUFFICIENT`.

## Known limitations and non-goals

Every estimate discloses that it is a within-stint association rather than pure physical degradation; fuel burn and track evolution are not separated; traffic/dirty air, driver management, energy deployment and local weather beyond configured exclusions are not modelled; and reconstructed tyre age does not establish physical tyre-set identity.

V2.2.3 does not add uncertainty, Gaussian assumptions, confidence percentages, outlier classification, pooling, fixed nuisance coefficients, pace/degradation APIs, frontend output, strategy simulation, recommendations, undercut/overcut logic, traffic loss, V2.2.4, V2.2.5, V2.3 or V2.4 work.

## V2.2.4 boundary

V2.2.4 should preserve these point estimators and add deterministic empirical robustness. Representative pace can expose included-lap empirical quantiles. Accepted tyre slopes should receive leave-one-observation-out slope ranges and sign-stability diagnostics; the four-point Monaco finding demonstrates why fragile fits must be visible. Any interval must be labelled empirical/resampled rather than Gaussian confidence, must use no observations beyond the cutoff, and must leave unavailable results unavailable.
